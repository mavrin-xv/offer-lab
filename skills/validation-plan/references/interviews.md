# Interviews: Mom Test and switch interviews

## The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick, 2013)

Three rules:

1. Talk about their life, not your idea.
2. Ask about specifics in the past, not opinions about the future.
3. Talk less, listen more.

Good questions:

- "Tell me about the last time your counts were wrong. What happened?"
- "What did you do about it? What did that cost?"
- "What have you tried? Why did you stop?"
- "Who else deals with this? Can you introduce me?"
- "Can I see how you do it now?"

Bad questions (they invite compliments): "Would you use this?", "Do you think it is a good idea?", "How much would
you pay?"

Signals that count are commitments: their time (a second meeting, a screen share), their reputation (an introduction
to a peer or their boss) and their money (a deposit, a pre-order). Compliments, "keep me posted" and "sounds great"
are not signals. Write them down as zero.

After each interview, note: the pain in their words, what they do now, what it costs, and the commitment they gave
(or "none").

## Switch interview (Jobs to be Done)

Talk to people who recently switched to any answer for this job (a new app, a new bookkeeper, a spreadsheet they
built). Walk the timeline: first thought, the event that made them look, what they compared, the moment they decided,
the first use.

Map the four forces:

| Force | Question |
|---|---|
| Push (the old way hurts) | What made the old way stop working? |
| Pull (the new way attracts) | What did you hope the new one would do? |
| Anxiety (the new way worries) | What almost stopped you? |
| Habit (the old way holds) | What did you miss about the old way? |

A switch happens when push and pull beat anxiety and habit. Your page has to raise the first two and answer the last
two. Anxieties map to guarantees; habits map to "we do it for you".

## How many

5 to 10 problem interviews per niche before trusting the pattern. If 10 in a row do not name the pain unprompted,
`reality_check.py` gives KILL.
