# Angles: 8 generators and a 7-point gate

An angle is the one idea the page and the pitch lead with, about 10 words. Positioning decides what is true; the
angle decides what to say first. The generators below are adapted from the positioning-angles skill in
boring-marketing-skills (MIT, linked in the README credits), with the awareness stages from Eugene Schwartz's
*Breakthrough Advertising* (1966).

## First: how aware is the buyer?

| Stage | The buyer | The angle should |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | does not see the problem | show the cost they already pay without knowing |
| Problem aware | feels the pain, has no answer | name the pain in their words |
| Solution aware | knows answers exist, not yours | say why your kind of answer is better |
| Product aware | knows you, has not bought | remove the last doubt (guarantee, proof, price) |
| Most aware | ready | give the offer and the deadline, if there is a real one |

## The 8 generators

1. **Contrarian:** say the opposite of what the market repeats, and prove it. ("Sync apps do not fix counts. Counting does.")
2. **Unique mechanism:** name the method that makes the result happen. ("The 3-pass count match.")
3. **Transformation:** before and after in the buyer's numbers.
4. **Enemy:** a shared enemy that is not a named rival (a habit, a blind spot, a hidden cost). ("Returns that never get recounted.")
5. **Speed or ease:** first result fast, or nothing for them to do.
6. **Specificity:** one narrow buyer, said out loud. ("For shops with 200+ SKUs on two or more channels.")
7. **Social proof:** what peers already did, only with real, permitted proof.
8. **Risk reversal:** lead with the guarantee when doubt is the main blocker.

Write 2 options per generator that fits, then gate them.

## The 7-point gate

An angle passes only when all 7 are true:

1. A buyer at the chosen awareness stage understands it in one read.
2. It is specific: a number, a buyer, a mechanism or a timeframe.
3. It is true today and you can show the proof.
4. A rival could not put it on their page word for word.
5. It points at value the buyer said they want, not a feature you like.
6. It is about the buyer's result, not about you.
7. It survives "so what?" from a busy buyer.

Mark each option pass or fail per line. Keep the passing ones; a human picks.
