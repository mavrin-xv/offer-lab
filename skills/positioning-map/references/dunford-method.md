# Dunford's method, in short

Source: April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* (2019). This file is a working summary built from public notes on the
book (Commoncog's summary and Yevgeniy Brikman's notes, linked in the README credits). Read the book for the full
argument. Where this pack adds something, it says so.

## The 10 steps

1. Understand the customers who love you. Start from your best buyers, not from everyone.
2. Form a positioning team. Positioning touches sales, product and marketing, so the people who own them decide together.
3. Align on positioning notation: agree that the current positioning may be an accident and that you can change it.
4. List your true competitive alternatives: what buyers would do without you, including nothing or hiring someone.
5. Isolate your unique attributes: what you have that the alternatives do not.
6. Map the attributes to value themes: what each attribute lets the buyer do, grouped into a few themes.
7. Determine who cares a lot: the buyer characteristics that make someone value those themes most.
8. Find a market frame of reference that puts your strengths at the centre.
9. Layer on a trend, carefully, only if it makes the value more obvious now.
10. Capture the positioning so it can be shared, then turn it into a sales story and copy.

## The three category styles

| Style | When | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Head to head | the category exists and buyers know it; you want to be the leader in it | you need to beat the leader on what the category is judged by |
| Big fish in a small pond | the category exists, and you win in a sub-segment with special needs | the segment must be big enough and easy to name |
| Create a new category | no existing frame fits your value | expensive and slow; buyers have to learn the frame first |

For a first offer from a small team, the middle style is usually the cheapest: "inventory cleanup for multi-channel
shops" rather than "inventory software".

## What this pack adds

- **Claim triage** (`claim_triage.py`): counts how many rivals already make each claim, so "unique" is checked, not felt.
- **Complaints as input**: complaints buyers write about rivals point at value nobody delivers yet.
- **Doing nothing as a pack**: the status quo has claims too ("free", "we know our own stock").
