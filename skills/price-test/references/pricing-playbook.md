# Pricing playbook

Collected from the pricing skill in coreyhaines31/marketingskills (MIT), Madhavan Ramanujam and Georg Tacke's
*Monetizing Innovation* (2016), and value-based pricing guides (links in the README credits). The thresholds are
heuristics.

## Value-based, not cost-plus

Start from what the result is worth to the buyer, then check that your cost leaves a margin. Cost-plus pricing
(your hours plus a markup) caps you at your own rate and tells the buyer nothing about value.

Value-capture check: price divided by the value you deliver per period. Under about 10% often leaves money on the
table; over about 30% needs strong proof. These are rules of thumb from practice, not research findings.

## The three buckets

| Bucket (per month) | Typical buyer | Sales motion |
|---|---|---|
| about 10 | individuals, very small shops | self-serve, no calls, low support |
| about 100 | small businesses | a page, a short call or a trial, light onboarding |
| about 1000 | teams, established businesses | calls, a proposal, a pilot, a named contact |

A service from a small team almost never works in the first bucket. If the math only works there, the niche or the
delivery is wrong.

## Good, better, best

- Good: the core result, done by them or done with them.
- Better (the target): the result done for them, plus the item that kills the biggest objection.
- Best: more scope or speed, priced high enough to make Better look sensible. Some buyers will take it.

Tiers differ by outcome or how much you do, never by a long feature grid.

## Talk about price early

*Monetizing Innovation* argues for discussing willingness to pay before the product is built, so the product is
designed around a price buyers accept. For a service: put the price in the first conversations and watch the
reaction. An offer described in words at a real price is enough to learn from.

## Price changes

- Existing buyers: 60 to 90 days notice, a stated grandfather period, the reason in one sentence.
- New buyers: the new price at once.
- Raise in steps you can test (one step, watch the close rate for a few weeks) rather than one large jump.

## Gotchas

- Free trials for a done-for-you service train buyers to expect free work. A paid pilot with a guarantee is safer.
- Monthly and annual at once: offer annual only when the value is clearly ongoing.
- Odd endings (449 instead of 450) matter less in services than in retail. Use whichever is easier to say on a call.
